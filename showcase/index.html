Heartwood Website redesign · Design concepts, October 2026

Website redesign

A new home for Heartwood online

Below is the design I recommend, followed by five alternative directions. Each one is a working mockup you can click through on a computer or a phone. These are about look and feel. Wording, photos and details are stand-ins for now and can all change.

Recommended

Living Tree

The whole network, drawn as one tree.

A hand-cut paper illustration of a single tree in a meadow, with its roots spreading out below ground. Every leaf opens a piece of Heartwood: a campaign, a gathering, a news story, a member group. It says what Heartwood is (many people and groups, one living thing) before anyone reads a word, and it gives visitors a reason to explore.

Things to try

  • Tap or click any leaf to open that part of the site.
  • Use “Every leaf matters” to light up leaves by topic.
  • Scroll down past the roots for the work, the gatherings and the network.

Other directions

If the tree isn’t quite right

Five other directions, each with its own personality. Hover over a preview to scroll through the page, or open any of them to click around.

Alternative 1

Evergreen

Photographic, calm, immersive

Deep forest greens and full-width woodland photography under a big, warm headline. It feels like walking into the woods, and it reads as an established conservation voice.

Open Evergreen (opens in a new tab)
Alternative 2

Rally

Bold, urgent, action-first

Tall headlines, a bright lime highlight and a “Find your focus” section that points each visitor to a campaign fast. Built for momentum and getting people involved.

Open Rally (opens in a new tab)
Alternative 3

Fieldnotes

Editorial, quiet, literary

Laid out like a nature journal or a small magazine, with a centered masthead, generous serif type and rust-red accents. A natural fit for longer stories and the Heartbeat.

Open Fieldnotes (opens in a new tab)
Alternative 4

Atlas

Map-first, practical, field-guide

Opens with a map of the region: pick a state to see the member groups already organizing there. Topographic linework and field-guide labels put the network itself front and center.

Open Atlas (opens in a new tab)
Alternative 5

Rings

Dark, warm, story-led

Heartwood’s history told as the growth rings of a tree, from its beginnings in 1991 to the network today, in warm gold on deep brown. It leads with the story of how Heartwood came to be.

Next step

How to choose

  1. Click through a few

    Open the ones that catch your eye and scroll all the way down. If you can, look at them on your phone as well, since that’s where many visitors will meet the site.

  2. Pick a favorite

    Reply to my email with the name of the design you like best, whether that’s Living Tree or one of the alternatives.

  3. Point out what to keep

    If a detail from another design caught your eye, like Atlas’s state map or the timeline in Rings, mention it. Pieces can often be carried over.
