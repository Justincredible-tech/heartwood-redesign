A regional forest protection network · Est. 1991

Thirty-five rings, and still growing.

Heartwood is the dense core of a tree, the part that holds the rest of it up. Since 1991 we have tried to be that for the grassroots forest movement in the eastern United States: people helping people protect the places they love.

The forest that was
It is said that a squirrel could roam from the Eastern Seaboard to the Mississippi River, tree to tree, without ever touching the ground.

Eastern North America was once blanketed by a hardwood forest. Much of it has been cleared for farms and cities. What remains is mostly isolated fragments of public land.

Those fragments still shelter wildlife, purify air and water, moderate the climate, and give people somewhere beautiful to be. Heartwood exists to protect and restore them.
